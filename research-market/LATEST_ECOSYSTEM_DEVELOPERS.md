# ECOSYSTEM_DEVELOPERS
Status: BUILT
Reconciled-Against: HIP marketplace developer TAM analysis + WhitePaper competitive section, 2026-07-06

Research on 40 existing household-market companies across 9 categories that would benefit from
HIP as a shared household-AI kernel (household identity, persistent shared context, privacy-first
custody, institutional integration). Each: current scale, current limitation, HIP unlock, source.

Version: 40-company set, current to mid-2026. Supersedes the earlier 36-company version.

This is the DEMAND-SIDE validation for HIP's developer marketplace: real companies, already
funded and operating, each hitting a wall that household-scoped AI would remove.

---

## Category 1: Family coordination and household management

**1. Cozi** — family calendar, lists, meal planning. 20M+ members (2017 reporting). Acquired by OurFamilyWizard from Dotdash Meredith, 2022. Limit: shared app, not a persistent household intelligence layer; manual entry, no cross-app memory or institutional authorizations. Unlock: household ops interface over persistent family context, school/health/eldercare feeds.

**2. Maple** — AI-assisted family OS (calendars, chores, lists, meals). $3.5M seed (Inspired Capital). Closest to HIP pattern but app-scoped; no durable identity layer, enclave custody, or broad institutional integration. Unlock: Maple as front end, HIP as memory/identity/consent/routing substrate.

**3. FamilyWall** — shared organizer (calendar, lists, location, docs, meals, expenses, messaging). ~100K downloads / ~$200K monthly revenue (Sensor Tower). Limit: productivity app, no knowledge graph or permission model. Unlock: trusted command surface over household context.

**4. OurHome** — chores, shopping, rewards, collaboration. No reliable current count. Limit: task/chore oriented, no cross-domain memory or consent. Unlock: attach chores to broader household context.

**5. Nori (Domus Next / SuperNori)** — AI family coordination agent. 200K+ families (Jul 2026), 9.4 AI interactions/family/day, 23.2 shared tasks/household. Directly in the lane but no HIP-like trust boundaries, institutional integration, or durable custody. Unlock: HIP as secure household memory substrate underneath. NOTE: also a direct competitor (see #37).

**9. Ianacare** — family caregiver support. $12.1M Series A (2022), 50K+ caregivers; AARP/Elevance partnerships. Limit: needs persistent permissioned context across family/paid care/providers/benefits. Unlock: privacy+identity layer without collapsing roles into one account.

**10. Lotsa Helping Hands** — volunteer/family care coordination communities. No reliable count. Limit: organizes volunteers, not an intelligent care memory. Unlock: permissioned community-care layer around a household.

**11. Zingage** — AI home-care ops for agencies. $12.5M seed (Bessemer, 2025), 400+ agencies, 50K caregivers, 10M+ visits/yr. Limit: agency ops, not the family household context layer. Unlock: connect agency workflows to authorized family context.

## STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS

1. **Direct competitors are real and funded.** Nori 200K families, Ohai $9.99/mo, Luffu w/ Fitbit pedigree, Hearth. HIP is not entering an empty category. Differentiator is operator-edge custody + institutional integration + enclave-grade privacy, which none have.

2. **Direct pricing comps exist.** Ohai $9.99/mo, Khanmigo $4/mo. HIP Standard $9.99 validated vs a live competitor.

3. **Demand side of the marketplace is validated.** 40 funded companies, each hitting the same wall (no household identity/persistent context/custody/institutional integration). These are the first-wave app-layer partners.

4. **Two incumbents to watch:** b.well (OpenAI/ChatGPT health connectivity) and Life360 (97.8M MAU, public). These have distribution to move into HIP's space. b.well + OpenAI is the sharpest competitive signal.

5. **Category structure for the marketplace:** 9 categories map to HIP's application catalog verticals (family coord, eldercare, health, finance, smart home, safety, education, insurance, emerging). This is the seed taxonomy for the "17 platform applications" framing.
