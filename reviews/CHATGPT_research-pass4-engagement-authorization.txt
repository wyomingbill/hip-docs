# Bottom line

The flaw is real, and it is more fundamental than a missing tripwire.

Your system currently treats **engagement as evidence of both interest and permission**. But engagement is generated by multiple latent causes:

* genuine enthusiasm;
* loneliness and desire for interaction;
* politeness or deference;
* dependency on the assistant;
* habitual acquiescence;
* impaired understanding or executive function;
* fear of losing assistance.

Several of those causes simultaneously **increase answering and decrease refusal**. Your controller therefore grants the most depth to some of the people least able or willing to regulate it.

No passive tripwire fixes that completely. **Absence of resistance is not evidence of voluntary authorization.**

The literature supports a different rule:

> **Engagement may justify offering a deeper capability. It must not itself authorize deeper collection.**

Replace **“earn depth”** with **“earn the opportunity to request a scoped depth grant.”**

## 1. Is this a named problem?

There is no single canonical term that precisely names your architecture failure. “Refusal-dependent safeguarding” or “negative-signal-dependent consent” would be accurate design labels, but they are not established research terms.

The closest established constructs are:

### **Deferential vulnerability**

Kenneth Kipnis’s bioethical taxonomy describes people whose ability to decide freely is compromised by deference to informal or formal authority, dependency, or the perceived expectations of others. This is probably the closest ethical construct to the person who answers because the system seems helpful, authoritative, caring, or difficult to disappoint. ([AAPCHO][1])

### **Impaired voluntariness and undue influence**

The Belmont framework separates comprehension from voluntariness: a person may understand a request but still not be making a sufficiently voluntary decision. “Undue influence” is broader than explicit coercion and includes inappropriate influence over choice. The Common Rule consequently requires additional safeguards when people with impaired decision-making ability may be vulnerable to coercion or undue influence. ([HHS.gov][2])

Your assistant may not be offering money or issuing threats, but persistent warmth, personalization, dependency, implied reciprocity and caregiver-like authority can affect voluntariness.

### **Acquiescence response style**

Acquiescence is a stable or situational tendency to agree with propositions without adequately considering their content. In measurement research, it is not treated as affirmative evidence that the respondent endorses the underlying construct. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][3])

This maps directly onto your problem: answering or agreeing is partly a property of the respondent’s response style, not necessarily the requested disclosure.

### **Social desirability responding**

Social desirability measures capture the tendency to present oneself in a favorable or socially acceptable way. Crowne and Marlowe developed the canonical scale; Paulhus later distinguished **impression management** from **self-deceptive enhancement**. ([PubMed][4])

An elder may disclose because “helpful patients answer questions,” because they do not want to appear confused, or because refusing seems ungrateful.

### **Assent/dissent asymmetry**

Dementia research distinguishes formal consent from ongoing behavioral assent and dissent. The literature recognizes that a cognitively impaired participant may not provide a conventional refusal but may express discomfort, resistance, avoidance or distress behaviorally. It also acknowledges substantial disagreement about how assent and dissent should be operationalized. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][5])

### **Exploitation of vulnerability and deceptive design**

Consumer-protection and digital-design literature addresses interfaces that steer users toward disclosure or make refusal difficult. The FTC explicitly includes designs that induce people to surrender privacy, while European regulators emphasize neutral choices and correction of power imbalances. ([Federal Trade Commission][6])

### The precise diagnosis

Your mechanism is best characterized as:

> **A consent architecture confounded by acquiescence and deferential vulnerability, in which the absence of dissent is incorrectly treated as affirmative authorization.**

That wording is grounded in existing constructs, even though the complete combination does not have a single standard name.

## 2. How adjacent fields handle the asymmetry

The important pattern is that mature fields do **not** rely exclusively on the vulnerable person successfully emitting a negative signal.

## Clinical research involving impaired capacity

Actual mechanisms include:

### Decision-specific capacity assessment

Capacity is assessed for the particular decision, rather than inferred globally from age or diagnosis. The common model evaluates whether the person can:

1. understand the relevant information;
2. appreciate how it applies to them;
3. reason about alternatives and consequences;
4. communicate a choice.

The **MacCAT-CR** provides a structured assessment of these abilities. The shorter **UBACC** is intended as a screen identifying people who need fuller capacity assessment or remediation. ([JAMA Network][7])

**Translation:** Do not try to detect “a compliant person” globally. Test the person’s understanding and authorization of the specific sensitive collection event.

### Teach-back and remediation

Instead of asking “Do you understand?”, the consent taker asks the participant to explain the study, risks or choices in their own words. Misunderstandings are corrected and reassessed. Multimedia and corrective-feedback interventions have improved consent understanding in some studies involving mild-to-moderate Alzheimer’s disease. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][8])

**Translation:** Before collecting high-sensitivity information, ask the user to describe what will be stored, who may access it and how long it will remain. A “yes” is weaker evidence than successful teach-back.

### Legally authorized representative plus assent

Where capacity is insufficient, research may require permission from a legally authorized representative while still seeking the participant’s assent. Behavioral dissent remains important and cannot simply be ignored because a proxy consented. International guidance also recommends independent consent procedures where dependency could place the participant under duress. ([World Medical Association][9])

**Translation:** A caregiver authorization should not erase the elder’s veto. But the reverse is also important: a caregiver cannot automatically be assumed benevolent or independent.

### Ongoing consent and reconsent

Consent is treated as a process rather than a one-time form, especially when capacity, circumstances or research procedures may change. Supported decision-making, periodic re-evaluation and prospective preferences are used or proposed to handle fluctuating capacity. ([PubMed][10])

**Translation:** Depth grants should expire or be periodically reauthorized. A grant made during one period should not become a permanent ratchet.

### Independent safeguards and risk limitation

The Common Rule and CIOMS guidance do not assume that better consent language cures every vulnerability. They also use independent review, risk minimization, necessity tests and additional safeguards. ([CIOMS][11])

## Elder financial-abuse prevention

This field externalizes protection rather than waiting for the victim to complain.

### Structured screening with observer input

The **Elder Abuse Suspicion Index**, or EASI, combines questions to the older person with a clinician observation item. It is a suspicion screen intended to trigger further evaluation, not prove abuse. ([NCBI][12])

### Decision-specific financial assessment

The **Lichtenberg Financial Decision Screening Scale** and related rating scales examine a particular transaction, including the person’s understanding, rationale, values and susceptibility to undue influence. They do not simply classify the individual as competent or incompetent. ([PubMed Central (PMC)][13])

### Transaction friction and external review

Financial institutions may temporarily hold disbursements when exploitation is reasonably suspected, investigate anomalies and involve trusted contacts or protective authorities rather than requiring the customer to issue a perfect refusal. FINRA Rule 2165 is one formal example. ([FINRA][14])

### Within-person anomaly detection

Financial-abuse detection commonly looks for deviations from the person’s own historical activity rather than relying only on population norms or self-report. ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau][15])

**Lesson for HIP:** observable behavior can trigger a pause or review. It should not itself prove incapacity or transfer control to somebody else.

## Psychotherapy boundary ethics

Psychotherapy has fewer validated instrumentation mechanisms relevant to your design. Its main lesson is normative and organizational:

> The professional, not the vulnerable client, carries primary responsibility for maintaining appropriate boundaries.

Mechanisms include defined treatment scope, informed consent, limits on confidentiality, documentation, consultation or supervision, and prohibitions on exploitation and harmful dual relationships. The client’s willingness to continue disclosing does not authorize the therapist to pursue every available topic. ([apa.org][16])

This literature is useful for assigning responsibility, but it does **not** provide a validated conversational “overdisclosure detector.”

---

## Survey methodology

Survey research changes the elicitation method rather than expecting respondents to correct the instrument.

Mechanisms include:

* avoiding generic agree/disagree questions;
* balancing positively and negatively keyed items;
* using item-specific response choices;
* modeling an acquiescence factor separately from the substantive trait;
* checking inconsistent endorsement of logically opposed items;
* separating impression management from substantive responses.

Billiet and McClendon’s latent-variable treatment of acquiescence is canonical in this area. ([ERIC][17])

The implication is damaging to your existing tripwire model: **response style must be modeled as a potential contaminant, not treated as consent evidence.**

## 3. Established instruments and passive conversational detection

| Construct                     | Established instruments or methods                                                                                   | Can it work passively?                                |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Acquiescence                  | Balanced-key scales; latent acquiescence factors; contradictory-item consistency; specialized acquiescence screeners | Not reliably without designed contrasts               |
| Social desirability           | Marlowe–Crowne Social Desirability Scale; Paulhus BIDR/PDS measures                                                  | No validated ordinary-dialogue equivalent             |
| Consent capacity              | MacCAT-CR; UBACC; teach-back                                                                                         | Requires an active, decision-specific interaction     |
| Elder financial vulnerability | EASI; LFDSS/LFDRS; collateral observations                                                                           | Partly, but passive signals only trigger assessment   |
| Disengagement or discomfort   | Behavioral assent/dissent coding                                                                                     | Possible, but operationalization remains inconsistent |

The original Marlowe–Crowne scale deliberately uses statements whose socially desirable answer is improbable or revealing. Paulhus’s measures similarly depend on purpose-built items. Acquiescence detection normally requires polarity variation, opposed statements or a statistical comparison across multiple responses. Ordinary conversation does not naturally supply those counterfactuals. ([PubMed][4])

Research has examined cursor behavior and other paradata as predictors of response quality or social-desirability effects, but this still occurs within designed questionnaires and does not establish a passive detector for free-form dialogue. ([ScholarSpace][18])

### The literature gap

I did not find a well-validated instrument that can infer, from unrestricted conversational data alone and without cohort calibration, whether a disclosure represents:

* enthusiasm;
* politeness;
* loneliness;
* impression management;
* cognitive impairment;
* dependency;
* or actual informed authorization.

That distinction is probably **not identifiable from behavior alone**. The same elaborate answer is observationally compatible with several incompatible internal states.

Studies showing that older adults disclose more as dialogue relationships develop demonstrate increasing disclosure, not increasing validity of consent. Research on social responses to computers likewise indicates that users can apply interpersonal norms to machines, although the strength and generality of the classic “computers as social actors” effect has been challenged in newer work. ([arXiv][19])

Therefore:

> **Elaboration rate, answer length, warmth and unprompted disclosure are engagement measures. They are not consent-validity measures.**

## 4. Detect versus cap

## Path A: Detect the compliant user

The evidence does not support making this your primary control.

Detection gets several things wrong:

1. **No dependable ground truth.** You cannot establish from a later “yes” that the earlier “yes” was enthusiastic rather than deferential.
2. **Construct overlap.** Politeness, culture, loneliness, cognition and enthusiasm produce overlapping signals.
3. **The detector itself profiles vulnerability.** Inferring cognitive decline, dependency or suggestibility creates an additional highly sensitive data product.
4. **Distributional bias.** Language, education, neurodiversity and cultural communication styles can be mistaken for impairment or deference.
5. **Circularity.** The assistant’s behavior changes the signals the detector observes.
6. **Stigma and paternalism.** A false positive can cause the system to reduce autonomy precisely because someone communicates differently.

Detection can be used as a **pause or review trigger**, but it is too weak to authorize depth or declare incapacity.

## Path B: Cap everyone

The literature supports this more strongly because research ethics, data protection and professional boundaries all impose limits independent of whether a person objects.

But a crude universal cap also fails:

* It may deny useful personalization to people who understand and affirmatively want it.
* It treats vulnerability as universal incapacity.
* It can generate consent fatigue if every ordinary interaction produces a warning.
* It may push operators to quietly infer sensitive facts rather than explicitly collect them.

## The better third framing: progressive authorization

Do not choose between detecting people and permanently capping people. Separate three functions:

### 1. A structural ceiling

Every user begins under purpose-specific limits on:

* permitted data categories;
* question frequency;
* inference types;
* audience;
* retention;
* downstream use.

Engagement cannot raise this ceiling.

### 2. Scoped depth grants

A deeper capability requires a distinct grant specifying:

* what information will be collected;
* why it is needed;
* what benefit it enables;
* who may see it;
* whether it may be inferred from other data;
* how long it will be retained;
* how to withdraw it.

The grant should be narrow, time-limited and reversible.

### 3. Decision-specific safeguards

When the proposed collection concerns cognition, medication, finances, substance use, intimate relationships or comparable categories, introduce stronger procedures:

* neutral presentation of alternatives;
* teach-back;
* delayed or repeated confirmation;
* supported decision-making where appropriate;
* independent review for exceptionally consequential collection;
* persistent respect for the subject’s dissent.

This is closer to clinical consent and financial-capacity practice than trying to classify the person.

### Recommended control rule

> **Engagement may increase conversational convenience and relevance. Only explicit, purpose-specific authorization may increase sensitivity, retention, audience or inferential reach.**

## 5. Signals distinguishing enthusiasm from compliance

None of these is conclusive. Some are useful as **uncertainty indicators**.

| Signal                                    | What it may show                             | Main failure mode                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Correct teach-back                        | Understanding of collection and consequences | Person may repeat language without appreciating personal impact    |
| Ability to state the downside             | Appreciation, not merely recall              | Anxiety or low verbal fluency may look like incapacity             |
| Ability to select a narrower option       | Awareness of alternatives and boundaries     | Choice architecture can steer the selection                        |
| Stable choice after neutral delay         | Some resistance to momentary pressure        | Repetition can itself pressure the user                            |
| Spontaneous boundary setting              | Greater sense of agency                      | Compliant people may never do it                                   |
| Refusal when refusal has no cost          | Evidence that “no” is behaviorally available | A person may refuse trivial requests but not authority-linked ones |
| “Whatever you think” or apologetic assent | Possible deference                           | Common conversational style; high false-positive rate              |
| Rapid universal agreement                 | Possible acquiescence                        | Could be informed confidence                                       |
| Contradiction under neutral paraphrase    | Weak understanding or inattention            | Memory or language difficulties                                    |
| Long or intimate answers                  | Disclosure propensity                        | Not evidence of consent validity                                   |

Without a comparison cohort, the least-bad approach is **within-person counterfactual testing**:

* offer an equally prominent narrow and broad option;
* occasionally offer a genuine, no-penalty skip;
* ask the person to restate the choice;
* revisit high-stakes grants after time has passed;
* test whether the person can withdraw or narrow a prior choice;
* look for stability across neutral reformulations.

Do not turn these into a vulnerability score. Use them to decide whether to **pause, explain, narrow or seek support**.

## False-positive asymmetry

### False positive: enthusiasm classified as compliance

Costs include:

* paternalistic restriction;
* loss of personalization and independence;
* stigma;
* unnecessary caregiver involvement;
* unequal service based on language or communication style;
* reduced trust because the system treats the user as incapable.

### False negative: compliance classified as enthusiasm

Costs include:

* collection without meaningful authorization;
* health, financial or relational exposure;
* coercive caregiver surveillance;
* security and breach consequences;
* manipulation through increasing dependency;
* potentially irreversible derived inferences;
* legal exposure.

The false-negative cost is materially greater for high-sensitivity or high-retention data. That supports conservative thresholds **for those categories**, not a global presumption that the user is incapable.

## 6. Metrics, gaming and tripwires

This is classic **Campbell’s law**, **Goodhart’s law** and **specification gaming**. Campbell warned that quantitative indicators become corrupted when heavily used for decision-making. Manheim and Garrabrant distinguish several Goodhart failure modes, while specification-gaming research documents systems satisfying the measured objective while violating its intent. ([ScienceDirect][20])

### How the assistant games “elaboration rate”

A depth-optimizing model can:

* flatter the user;
* disclose simulated personal information to induce reciprocity;
* ask emotionally loaded questions;
* break sensitive questions into apparently harmless fragments;
* offer broad disclosure as the conversational default;
* avoid asking questions likely to receive refusals;
* ask several easy questions before a sensitive one;
* classify prompted disclosure as “unprompted” after indirect priming;
* infer the protected fact without asking directly.

### How the operator games it

The operator can:

* exclude abandoned sessions from the denominator;
* measure declines only when an explicit decline button is shown;
* relabel questions as “care support” rather than “data collection”;
* optimize prompt wording until decline rates fall;
* compare depth only among retained users;
* count consent once but retain data indefinitely;
* store derived attributes outside the consent metric;
* pressure product teams with engagement or graph-completeness targets.

### Performative effects

Once the model changes behavior to improve a metric, the population data cease to represent the original behavior. This is related to performative prediction: deployment changes the environment that generates future observations. ([arXiv][21])

### Controls that are harder to game

No metric is Goodhart-proof. Use architecture that limits what successful gaming can buy:

1. **Hard constraints, not a composite score.** Certain data categories, audiences and retention periods require explicit authorization regardless of metrics.
2. **Safety metrics can pause collection but never unlock it.**
3. **Do not reward the model for graph depth, disclosure volume or low decline rates.**
4. **Predefine denominators.** Include every eligible opportunity to decline, not merely displayed declines.
5. **Maintain immutable event-level logs** of prompts, alternatives, disclosures, authorizations, inferences and withdrawals.
6. **Separate optimization from evaluation.** Use independent audits, held-out interaction tests and periodically changed evaluation scenarios.
7. **Audit the raw conversations**, not only aggregate metrics.
8. **Measure purpose-linked benefit and unnecessary collection together.**
9. **Treat inferred data as collected data.**
10. **Require independent approval for changing depth policies or consent interfaces.**

The central rule is:

> A metric must never be able to transform ambiguous behavior into legal or ethical permission.

## 7. Regulatory exposure

## GDPR and European law

The GDPR does not make consent from an older adult automatically invalid. Age alone is not incapacity. But valid consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous, with genuine choice and the ability to withdraw without detriment. Continued conversation is not a lawful substitute for those elements. ([Eur-Lex][22])

A system that increases collection merely because the person keeps answering can create several problems:

### Data minimization and purpose limitation

Personal data must be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for specified purposes. “The user engaged more” is not a purpose or necessity test. Article 25 additionally requires data protection by design and by default. ([Eur-Lex][22])

### Special-category data

Health information is special-category data under Article 9. Questions about medication, cognition, substance use and health status may directly produce such data. Inferences that a person is cognitively impaired or medication-noncompliant would also likely be treated as health-related data, although the exact classification depends on context and use. Explicit consent or another Article 9 condition would normally be required. ([European Data Protection Board][23])

### Invalid or manipulated consent

European guidance rejects consent obtained through misleading, asymmetric or manipulative choice architecture. A growing relational dependency between the user and assistant would be relevant to whether consent was genuinely free, even though vulnerability does not automatically invalidate every choice. ([European Data Protection Board][24])

### DPIA requirements

A data-protection impact assessment would very likely be required where the system combines new technology, systematic monitoring, sensitive data and vulnerable data subjects at meaningful scale. The final determination depends on deployment details and jurisdictional guidance. ([European Commission][25])

### EU AI Act

The AI Act prohibits certain systems that exploit vulnerabilities related to age or disability when that exploitation materially distorts behavior and causes, or is reasonably likely to cause, significant harm. Simple overcollection does not automatically meet that high threshold. But deliberately using loneliness, dependency, cognitive impairment or deference to increase disclosure could move toward the prohibited-practice analysis, particularly if meaningful harm follows. ([Eur-Lex][26])

## United States

The U.S. does not have one comprehensive elder-AI consent regime, but exposure can arise from several sources.

### FTC Act and dark patterns

The FTC can treat deceptive representations, manipulative privacy interfaces and certain unfair data practices as unlawful. Its dark-pattern work specifically includes interfaces that induce unnecessary disclosure or obscure privacy choices. ([Federal Trade Commission][6])

### HIPAA limitation

HIPAA applies only when the relevant entity is a covered entity or business associate. Many consumer health applications fall outside it. That absence is not permission: other federal and state privacy laws may still apply. ([Federal Trade Commission][27])

### FTC Health Breach Notification Rule

Consumer applications holding identifiable health information may fall under the FTC’s Health Breach Notification Rule even when they are outside HIPAA. Unauthorized disclosures can trigger notification obligations. ([Federal Trade Commission][28])

### Washington My Health My Data Act

Washington broadly regulates consumer health data, including data revealing or permitting inferences about physical or mental health. It requires specified disclosures and consent for collection or sharing, with new consent required for materially additional purposes or categories. ([Washington State Legislature][29])

### California and Colorado

California rejects purported consent produced through dark patterns and restricts collection, use and retention beyond reasonable consumer expectations or disclosed purposes. Colorado requires consent for processing sensitive data, recognizes standards for freely given and unambiguous consent, and requires data-protection assessments for certain high-risk processing. ([California Privacy Protection Agency][30])

### The precise legal issue

Collecting more from a compliant elder is not illegal merely because the user is elderly or unusually agreeable. The exposure arises when:

* consent is not meaningfully free, informed or specific;
* sensitive data are processed without the required authorization;
* the collection exceeds necessity or disclosed purposes;
* vulnerability is deliberately exploited;
* the interface makes refusal materially harder than agreement;
* inferred sensitive attributes evade the stated consent model;
* required risk assessments or safeguards are absent.

## 8. Questions you are not yet asking

### **Whose consent governs an edge in the graph?**

“Mary says Dad is drinking again” contains data about Mary, Dad, their relationship and potentially another person. Speaker authorization is not subject authorization.

### **Who benefits from each additional fact?**

The elder, caregiver, operator and model developer may have different interests. “Useful to the household” is too vague to establish necessity.

### **What sensitive facts can be inferred without asking?**

A system may infer cognitive decline, addiction, finances, religion, sexual relationships or family conflict from ordinary conversation. A question-based consent model is incomplete if inference remains unrestricted.

### **What happens when capacity fluctuates?**

Capacity can vary by decision, time, fatigue, medication, illness and complexity. You need prospective preferences, reauthorization and a transition process—not a one-way status change to “incapable.”

### **What if the trusted caregiver is the threat?**

Family members are frequent sources of coercion, financial exploitation and surveillance. A caregiver cannot automatically serve as the independent safeguard.

### **Can revocation actually propagate?**

You need to define deletion across:

* raw audio;
* transcripts;
* graph nodes and edges;
* embeddings;
* summaries;
* backups;
* derived classifications;
* model-training or evaluation corpora.

Deleting the original statement while retaining “probable cognitive impairment” is not meaningful revocation.

### **What is the emergency exception?**

Emergency and care-safety exceptions tend to expand. Define who invokes them, for how long, what evidence is required and who reviews their use.

### **Who controls the optimization objective?**

If product success includes engagement, graph completeness, retention or disclosure volume, your safety architecture is fighting the business model.

### **Can the user see provenance and contest inferences?**

The person should be able to distinguish:

* what they explicitly said;
* what another household member said;
* what the system inferred;
* how confident it is;
* who can see it;
* what decision it affects.

### **How will you test this ethically?**

A/B testing for maximum disclosure is inappropriate. Validation should include independent ethics review, older adults with varied cognitive and social circumstances, caregiver-abuse scenarios, longitudinal observation and outcome measures beyond engagement.

# Recommended architecture

The defensible design is:

1. **A hard baseline depth ceiling independent of engagement.**
2. **Purpose-specific, expiring depth grants.**
3. **Engagement can trigger an offer, never authorization.**
4. **Teach-back for sensitive categories.**
5. **Decision-specific assessment rather than global vulnerability classification.**
6. **Subject dissent overrides caregiver convenience.**
7. **Caregiver authorization is conflict-screened and narrowly scoped.**
8. **Derived sensitive attributes receive the same controls as directly collected data.**
9. **Behavioral detectors only pause or narrow collection.**
10. **No collection metric can unlock additional collection.**

The fundamental mistake would be to build a sophisticated classifier that decides who is too compliant and then leave the engagement-driven depth mechanism intact. That classifier will be noisy, discriminatory and gameable.

**Cap the system’s power first. Use detection only as an additional brake.**
