### TIER-1 Design Summary

Third axis alongside validity (built) and trust (built). Heat tracks access recency only; moves independently of the other two axes.

**The invariant (non-negotiable):** Storage heat optimizes cost and latency only. It NEVER influences a governance decision. A cold fact still clears all disclosure gates if valid, in-scope, relevant, and trusted. Cold means slower to fetch (promote-on-access), never withheld. "We did not surface your shellfish allergy because it was cold" is the catastrophic failure this invariant prevents.

**What needs speccing before build:**
1. Access tracking per fact without becoming a hot-path cost itself
2. Promotion/demotion policy (promote-on-access immediate; decay time-based)
3. Tier boundaries: hot = in-memory/fast store; warm = primary graph; cold = cheap deep store (current: everything in Neo4j at one speed, no tiering)
4. Disclosure-blindness conformance test: fetch a deliberately-cold valid fact and assert it is admitted identically to a hot one (latency only differs)
5. Cold-fetch latency budget: promoted cold fact must return within acceptable window

**Rationale:** Mandatory at scale (thousands of facts/household x millions of households). Operator-native framing (tiered storage is the operator's world: CDN edge caching pattern). Moat argument: governed + cost-optimized storage labs cannot show (labs do not tier governed context because they do not govern context).

**Build sequencing:** DESIGN now; BUILD after dashboard-clarity demo lands. Dev sequence: baseline lock -> NDA cascade -> dashboard-clarity demo -> TIER-1 spec pass -> TIER-1 build.
